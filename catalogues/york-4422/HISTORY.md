# Historical notes and references

## Classical enumeration and names

Thomas Cope and Roger Colbeck obtained the 175 classes for two parties, four settings per observer and binary outputs. Their CH order is the basis for Y1 through Y175. The traditional identifiers used here follow Appendix A and the coefficient supplement in the paper by Enky Oudot, Jean-Daniel Bancal, Pavel Sekatski and Nicolas Sangouard. For every row, the permutation, outcome sign choices, possible party exchange and normalizing factor are supplied. Re-evaluation on all deterministic assignments identifies the full multilinear polynomial, including its constant and marginal terms. The catalogue result proves the enumeration of classical facets. [Cope and Colbeck, Phys. Rev. A 100, 022114 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.100.022114) · [Oudot et al., Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05636).

Nicolas Brunner and Nicolas Gisin tabulated four-setting inequalities and reported numerical quantum violations. Károly F. Pál and Tamás Vértesi compared numerical quantum strategies and upper bounds for many facets. The historical numerical work supplies context and nomenclature. The two proof obligations in this edition are a universal upper bound and physical saturation or approximation at exactly that value. [Brunner and Gisin (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.3362) · [Pál and Vértesi (2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1615).

E. Zambrini Cruzeiro and N. Gisin also published the 175-facet list and studied its qubit violations, noise resistance and detection efficiency. Those qubit calculations concern a prescribed local dimension. [Zambrini Cruzeiro and Gisin (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11820).

## Historical exact quantum values

CHSH is covered by Cirel’son's exact theorem. The correlation-only entries Y25 and Y74 coincide, up to a signed relabelling and CH normalization, with the two four-setting full-correlation facets solved analytically by Ben Li. The number I3322 denotes a different phenomenon: its unrestricted value is most naturally presented here as a path variational constant. The primary strict-value references and their separate roles are listed with the Y003 proof. A zero-upper positivity facet follows immediately from joint-probability nonnegativity. [Cirel’son (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00417500) · [Li, Sections 5.2–5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.08506) · [Coladangelo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06038) · [Pauwels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29734) · [Krohn-Grimberghe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29693) · [Connor, 2026 preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/411642957_Maximal_I_3322_Violation_Requires_Infinite_Local_Dimension).

## Methods and public computational contributions

The moment-matrix hierarchy of Navascués, Pironio and Acín supplies upper bounds in the commuting model. A numerical near-equality is distinct from an exact operator identity and matching strategy. Hrga, Klep and Povh study the A-series through moment and algebraic methods. Their finite-dimensional reconstructions and numerical checks should be interpreted according to the individual hypotheses and arithmetic of each result. [Navascués, Pironio and Acín](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.4290) · [Hrga, Klep and Povh](https://igorklep.github.io/files/Hrga_Klep_Povh_v3.pdf).

The Percivio Certified Science project publishes machine-checkable materials for the Pál–Vértesi J62 functional, corresponding to Y92. Its published interval is evidence of a different kind from an equality. The repository identifies its own computational contributions. This edition claims no prior discovery on the strength of a numerical match or an automated search. [Percivio, public certificate collection](https://github.com/Percivio-Ltd/certified-science).

Coladangelo describes a specific AI contribution to the I3322 theorem. In the author's account, interactive work with GPT 5.5 Pro produced a preliminary argument containing the main ideas. The author substantially revised and completed the proof, with assistance from GPT 5.6 Sol. Authorship and the mathematical claims here are attributed to the paper. [Coladangelo, version 2, “AI usage statement”](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06038v2).

The remaining bundled exact certificates are extracted from the user-organized predecessor project artifacts identified by file and archive hashes in [the mathematical provenance record](PROVENANCE.md). That record separates the published coefficient catalogue, exact prior theorems, local proof implementations and embedded third-party runtimes. Where an external strict exact-value proof has been identified, the individual entry cites it. Absence of such a citation is not a priority claim.

## Further numerical identifications and optimality methods

The matching-bound reports in Pál and Vértesi (2009), including $I_{4422}^{3}$, $I_{4422}^{4}$ and $I_{4422}^{16}$, are historical numerical identifications. The entry bibliographies use the narrower criterion of a located strict analytic-value proof. Absence from that list is not a priority claim for the value or physical strategy. Their 2010 work also studies higher-dimensional strategies and difficult four-setting cases. [2009 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1615) · [2010 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.3032).

H.-Y. Hsu, G. N. M. Tabia, K.-S. Chen, M.-E. Liu, T. Vértesi, N. Brunner and Y.-C. Liang, *Trading symmetry for Hilbert-space dimension in Bell-inequality violation*, Quantum Science and Technology **11**, 035003 (2026), studies party-exchange symmetry and dimension requirements, including symmetric 4422 facets. Tables 1, 5 and 6 report NPA and quantum-strategy computations. These earlier value and strategy identifications are credited without treating their printed decimals as exact certificates. [Version 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02893v4) · [Article](https://doi.org/10.1088/2058-9565/ae6bb1).

M. Araújo, I. Klep, A. J. P. Garner, T. Vértesi and M. Navascués, *First-Order Optimality Conditions for Non-Commutative Optimization Problems*, Foundations of Computational Mathematics (2026), develops noncommutative optimality conditions and applies the resulting relaxations to Bell problems. Section 6.3 includes numerical convergence and rank-loop tests for $I_{4422}^{20}$ (Y168). The general theory and that numerical evidence have different roles from an exact algebraic certificate for a particular Bell value. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-026-09761-x).

Ł. Czekaj, M. Pawłowski, T. Vértesi, A. Grudka, M. Horodecki and R. Horodecki, *Quantum advantage for distributed computing without communication* (2014), Eqs. (14)–(17), give an identity-game realization of $I_{4422}^{6}$ (Y153), a measurement construction and numerical agreement with an NPA upper bound. Their analytic CHSH-based argument for the separate $I_{\mathrm{add}}$ game concerns a different functional. The earlier strategy and numerical identification are therefore credited without using that argument as an exact upper-bound proof for Y153. [Author manuscript, version 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.0993v2).
