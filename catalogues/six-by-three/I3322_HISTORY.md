# Historical references for the three-setting inequality

The [entry](entries/02-I3322/PROOF.md) gives the unrestricted upper proof, explicit approaching strategies and its [exact-value bibliography](entries/02-I3322/REFERENCES.md). The present historical bibliography also covers the original facet, numerical investigations, related functionals, restricted strategies and parallel developments. A reported value for a restricted class has a different quantifier from the unrestricted quantum supremum.

## The facet and its conventional name

- M. Froissart, *Constructive generalization of Bell's inequalities*, Il Nuovo Cimento B **64**, 241–251 (1981). Early construction of the non-CHSH three-setting facet. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02903286).
- I. Pitowsky and K. Svozil, *Optimal tests of quantum nonlocality*, Phys. Rev. A **64**, 014102 (2001). Early polyhedral treatment including the bipartite three-setting experiment. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.64.014102).
- C. Śliwa, *Symmetries of the Bell correlation inequalities*, Phys. Lett. A **317**, 165–168 (2003). Shows that the genuinely three-setting orbit is unique in the bipartite binary scenario; this is separate from the paper's tripartite list. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0375-9601(03)01115-0).
- D. Collins and N. Gisin, *A relevant two qubit Bell inequality inequivalent to the CHSH inequality*, J. Phys. A **37**, 1775–1787 (2004). Source for the modern notation and for the relevance of this facet to two-qubit nonlocality. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0306129).
- J. Jesus and E. Zambrini Cruzeiro, *Tight Bell inequalities from polytope slices*, Phys. Rev. A **108**, 052220 (2023). Places the lifted facet in the complete six-versus-three scenario. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.108.052220).

## How the quantum problem developed

- M. Navascués, S. Pironio and A. Acín, *A convergent hierarchy of semidefinite programs characterizing the set of quantum correlations*, New J. Phys. **10**, 073013 (2008). Moment relaxations give upper approximations in the commuting model; a finite-level decimal does not by itself prove exact attainment. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/10/7/073013).
- K. F. Pál and T. Vértesi, *Quantum bounds on Bell inequalities*, Phys. Rev. A **79**, 022120 (2009); *Maximal violation of the I3322 inequality using infinite-dimensional quantum systems*, Phys. Rev. A **82**, 022116 (2010). The latter gives a finite-dimensional family of increasing size and the conjectured limiting behavior. [2009 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1615), [2010 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.3032).
- T. Vidick and S. Wehner, *More nonlocality with less entanglement*, Phys. Rev. A **83**, 052310 (2011). Their analytic restriction on maximally entangled states of any finite dimension explains why optimizing only over those states loses information. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.83.052310).
- N. Gigena and J. Kaniewski, *Quantum value for a family of I3322-like Bell functionals*, Phys. Rev. A **106**, 012401 (2022). Solves a different branch of a parameterized family and investigates numerically the branch containing the original inequality. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.106.012401).
- N. Mghirbi, *Proof-carrying exact quantum bounds for the I3322 Bell inequality* (2026), archived preprint. Certified rational bounding evidence. Coladangelo's discussion of concurrent work also identifies a separate deposit by Mghirbi, *Critical Bellman–Jacobi dynamics for reversible Bell functionals*. [Bounding certificates](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21477901), [second deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21910040), [concurrent-work discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06038v2#S1.SS2).
- S. Douglas, *The I3322 quantum value is attained spatially but not in finite dimension*, [arXiv:2609.05555v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05555v2), revised 11 September 2026. Version 2 gives finite-dimensional nonattainment, normal spatial attainment and dimension-complexity results; it replaces an unsupported component-identification step with a weighted-flow argument. The [v4.1.0 proof archive](https://github.com/Apsiape/i3322-exact-wall/releases/tag/v4.1.0) and [versioned deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22698978) should be distinguished from superseded exact-identification claims. The complete analytic proof is not Lean-formalized; the author describes the narrower scope of the formal checks. This is related work rather than a proof dependency of this release.

## Exact characterizations and computational contributions

Pauwels proves the finite-dimensional path reduction, Coladangelo gives an unrestricted upper argument and a normal infinite-dimensional attaining construction, and Krohn-Grimberghe presents an alternative computer-assisted analytic identification with separately stated written premises. Connor gives a further written measure-variational proof. The latter two characterizations are not used as dependencies of this repository's upper-bound and approximation argument. Their individual theorems, dates, arXiv versions and proof sections appear in the [entry bibliography](entries/02-I3322/REFERENCES.md).

Pauwels describes Codex's part in the initial derivation, Claude's part in subsequent scrutiny and the scope of a Lean formalization. Coladangelo identifies this paper in the author's list of results obtained primarily with AI. Krohn-Grimberghe provides a contribution statement explaining the automated review and separates the written functional-analytic arguments from computer-assisted finite checks. Douglas likewise describes which parts of the work were formally checked. These statements document how the cited authors carried out their work. The mathematical arguments and the independently evaluable path strategy are included in this repository. [Pauwels](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29734v1), [Coladangelo's publication list](https://andreacoladangelo.com/publications/), [Krohn-Grimberghe](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29693v1), [Douglas](https://github.com/Apsiape/i3322-exact-wall).
