# Historical context and bibliography guide

This combined edition brings together three catalogues with different
classification histories. A classical facet classification, a numerical
quantum optimization and an exact dimension-independent quantum-value proof
are different achievements. The references below are organized around that
distinction. The complete annotations and bibliographic metadata remain in
the linked catalogue histories; they are part of this distribution.

## The tripartite catalogue

The Sliwa catalogue concerns three parties with two binary measurements each.
The original enumeration and the earlier work of Pitowsky and Svozil define
the classical setting. Later studies by Lopez-Rosa, Xu and Cabello and by
Vallins, Sainz and Liang reported quantum values and numerical comparisons.
The catalogue survey distinguishes an analytical expression reported after
numerical agreement from a complete exact global proof.

Prior strict results include the CHSH/Mermin families, explicit class-level
sum-of-squares proofs, and subsequent exact calculations for specified
full-correlation representatives. The per-entry documentation specifies
which literal or equivalent class each citation establishes; it does not
extend a result for one class to all 46. The supplied collection gives exact
value definitions and upper-and-attainment materials for every numbered
representative.

## The two bipartite catalogues

The (6,3,2,2) catalogue contains seven facet classes. Its historical
classification is discussed alongside the work of Jesus and Zambrini
Cruzeiro. The present directory includes an exact finite enumeration and a
written completeness proof, separately from the quantum-value arguments.
The traditional I4322 labels are retained. CHSH has its established sharp
unrestricted bound; the I4322 and I5322 documentation distinguishes the
historical classification and numerical studies from the exact proof
materials supplied here.

The York volume contains all 175 (4,4,2,2) representatives in its frozen
probability convention, together with traditional-name correspondences.
Its survey distinguishes historical facet lists, exact special cases,
numerical bounds and the included general certificate constructions. Four
representatives remain unresolved. This combined edition does not add
interval progress statements to their entries or its general history.

## I3322 and model distinctions

The I3322 literature includes early facet discovery, finite-dimensional
optimization, higher-dimensional path strategies and subsequent proofs
identifying an unrestricted variational value. The supplied proof uses an
explicit Jacobi-path definition and a legal approximating family. Its upper
argument and the cited attainment results have their scopes stated separately.
Equality of suprema does not imply attainment at a finite dimension.

The detailed references distinguish Pauwels's path comparison and
finite-dimensional nonattainment from Coladangelo's treatment of the normal
spatial model. Krohn-Grimberghe's alternative implicit identification and
Connor's alternative measure-variational argument are documented with their
own stated premises. They are not silently substituted for the local proof
or made into missing computational dependencies.

## Computational and automated contributions

The catalogue histories preserve relevant computational and automated
contributions reported by the original authors, including current exact
certificate work, interval certification and formalization projects. Such
attribution is part of the scholarly record. It does not identify every
numerical optimization or software output with a proof of an exact quantum
supremum. See the original references and their contribution statements in
the linked bibliographies.

This publication makes no worldwide priority claim merely because a
class-specific earlier proof was not identified. Repeated bipartite entries
represent shared mathematics in different catalogues and are not counted
as new independent results. The root dispatcher and the coefficient
correspondence checks are organizational and reproducibility components;
they do not replace the upper and attainment arguments in the entries.

## Recent work in adjacent Bell scenarios

L.-L. Sun, J. Wang and S. Yu, *Exact Identification of Quantum Correlations in the Minimal Bell Scenario*, [arXiv:2610.34364v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34364v1), studies the boundary of the bipartite two-setting, two-outcome quantum set. M.-E. Liu, A. Zingarofalo, V. Barizien and J.-D. Bancal, *All Bell inequalities with a quantum advantage in the minimal scenario are self-testing*, [arXiv:2611.06997v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06997v1), studies the maximizing correlations and supporting inequalities in that same minimal scenario. Their scope does not supply exact values for general four-setting facets. They are relevant geometric background, not substitutes for the separate entry proofs.

## Rational certification of numerical Gram data

Y. Naceur, J. Wang, V. Magron and A. Acín, *Certified bounds on optimization problems in quantum theory*, arXiv:2512.17713v2 (26 October 2026), develop rational rounding, affine projection and positivity restoration for noncommutative certificates, including sparse and symmetry-adapted formulations. Section 5.1 applies the method to A2–A89. A certified outer bound and a matching exact quantum supremum are separate conclusions; this work is credited as prior certification theory and a catalogue application.
